# Internal smoothing uncertainty coverage study

This is a simulation study of the retained ML/REML/fREML prototype. It adds no public sampling option. The scenarios and seeds were chosen before inspecting coverage. The Gaussian and family pilot runs checked execution, rather than selecting cases on their coverage results.

The main study uses 200 independently simulated responses per case, holding covariates fixed. The same response is fitted with gam ML and REML and non-discrete bam ML, REML and fREML. Sixteen cases cover Gaussian data with known or estimated dispersion, small samples, weak and absent smooths, shared and partly fixed smoothing parameters, a tensor product, Poisson and sparse Poisson counts, binomial counts and rare Bernoulli outcomes, Gamma, negative-binomial, beta and Student-t responses. These are representative ordinary and extended families, not a calibration test of every supported family or link. Censored, ordinal, grouped, Tweedie and quasi cases are not included. NCV, general families and discrete bam remain outside the prototype.

The fixed truth is a natural cubic spline in the fitted basis, using the same supplied knots. Tensor truth is a product of functions in the two marginal bases. A separate geometry test verifies the exact linear predictor, constrained first smooth and its derivative. Gaussian observations have known unequal precisions and a true dispersion of 0.25; only the known-dispersion case supplies that scale to the fitter. Other cases estimate dispersion and scalar family parameters as usual. All cases have a known offset. Binomial counts use success/failure matrices with different trial counts. The family parameters are fixed at their estimates within every prototype mixture.

Four pointwise quantities are evaluated at eleven x values from 0 to 1, with z = 0.5 where present: the complete linear predictor, the first constrained smooth, that smooth's derivative with respect to x, and the expected response. The derivative is on the smooth's linear predictor scale, not the derivative of the expected response. For a tensor product it is the partial derivative with respect to x. Expected-response intervals use a monotone inverse link. These are intervals for functions, not future observations or simultaneous bands. For binomial fits, response mean denotes the fitted success probability, matching predict(type = "response"), rather than the expected number of successes.

The three methods use the fitted conditional coefficient covariance, mgcv's unconditional covariance, and the prototype mixture. Conditional and unconditional quantiles are calculated analytically from their Gaussian coefficient distributions. Mixture quantiles average conditional Gaussian CDFs over 256 antithetic draws of free log smoothing parameters, then solve for probabilities 0.025, 0.5 and 0.975. This avoids adding coefficient-draw Monte Carlo error to every coverage result; it uses the actual prototype conditional evaluator. It does not change the package sampler or its random numbers. Missing unconditional covariance is an error, not a request to substitute conditional covariance. The unconditional correction is used exactly as mgcv supplies it. It is not a moment-matched version of this mixture: its curvature handling differs, and extended-family gam fits can include derivatives for estimated family parameters. Differences between the procedures therefore do not isolate the effect of changing smoothing parameters alone.

Two further checks were specified before the main results: 1,024 mixture components for the first 20 replicates of the known-dispersion Gaussian, weak Gaussian, Poisson, negative-binomial and beta cases, across all five configurations; and 10,000 actual prototype coefficient draws for replicate 1 of the known-dispersion Gaussian, Poisson, negative-binomial and beta cases, with gam REML and bam fREML. The latter uses 4,096 integration components as a reference and also checks actual conditional and unconditional coefficient draws against their analytic intervals. Failures remain failures; there are no replacement datasets, discarded smoothing draws or clipped smoothing values.

After the main sparse-Poisson and rare-binomial coverage results became available, an additional resolution check was added for those two cases: the first 20 replicates, all five configurations and 1,024 components. This is a follow-up prompted by the observed undercoverage, not one of the checks specified before inspecting results. It uses the same datasets and records every numerical failure.

Coverage is reported separately for nine interior points and two boundaries. Its Monte Carlo standard error is the standard deviation of each dataset's fraction covered divided by the square root of the number of returned intervals. Grid points are not counted as independent simulations. At 95% pointwise coverage, 200 independent datasets give a standard error of about 1.5 percentage points. This initial screen can reveal substantial miscalibration, but cannot reliably resolve changes of one percentage point. Paired method differences use only the same datasets where all three methods return intervals and use the standard deviation of within-dataset coverage differences. Availability and coverage among returned intervals are separate results; returned-and-covered fractions also retain failures in the denominator.

Fitting failures, unavailable covariances, failed setup reconstruction and invalid sampled smoothing values are recorded. Native fit convergence is checked before constructing intervals. The raw fit and sampler status table also retains warnings and the largest fitted log-smoothing standard deviation. Numerical failures in diffuse smoothing approximations are part of the assessment, not missing simulations to be replaced.

Raw checkpoints are outside the package and protected by a protocol, package versions, commit identifier and source hashes. Independent deterministic seeds index the case and replicate before scheduling. The runner uses a named, scoped mirai compute profile with automatic cleanup and five datasets per checkpoint. Worker count is absent from the statistical protocol because scheduling must not alter results. Completed batches can be resumed only with an identical protocol and identical source hashes.

This evaluates approximate estimation-uncertainty propagation at fixed fitted nuisance parameters. Repeated-simulation coverage does not turn the construction into integration of a joint Bayesian posterior. Public integration remains a separate decision after assessment.

The study used mirai 2.7.3 daemon maps with RNGkind("L'Ecuyer-CMRG", "Inversion", "Rejection"). The runner and actual-draw checks both use these maps. Set that generator explicitly before calling the helper functions directly in a fresh R process; the same integer seed with a different generator produces different data. The standalone tests set the study generator. Direct serial and parallel reruns of the failed weak/null cases reproduced their saved main-study intervals and errors exactly, after removing elapsed times.
