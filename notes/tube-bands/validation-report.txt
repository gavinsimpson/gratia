The work was completed in these stages:

1. Implement Gaussian process geometry, a positive-semidefinite covariance
   factor, numerical integration of spherical curve length, and a critical
   value from the two-sided threshold-crossing bound.
2. Recover the fitted prediction basis for ordinary cubic cr, cs, ps, tp, and
   ts smooths, preserving mgcv's fitted constraints and column transformations.
   Use analytic basis derivatives through order three. Thin plate knot
   subsampling does not truncate the fitted covariate domain.
3. Add smooth, first/second derivative, and factor-by difference adapters.
   Preserve the selected coefficient covariance, overall smooth uncertainty,
   the joint contrast covariance, and constant group contributions. Evaluate
   finite differences by exact integration on each cubic piece to avoid
   subtracting nearly equal predictions. Preserve the requested mathematical
   finite-difference operator, stencil, and step.
4. Add domain and quadrature controls, per-curve metadata, public documentation,
   the pkgdown reference entry, release notes under New features, and plotting
   integration. Saved estimates retain their computed interval limits.
5. Validate analytic geometry, fitted matrices, adapters, edge cases, conditional
   coverage, repeated fits, performance, package examples, and rendered plots.

Calibration and assumptions

For uncertainty design d(x), covariance V = L L', and
u(x) = L'd(x) / sqrt(d(x)' V d(x)), integrate kappa = integral ||u'(x)|| dx.
For K regular components, solve

  2 K Pr(N(0,1) > c) + kappa exp(-c^2/2) / pi = 1 - level.

This counts endpoints and threshold crossings and bounds the two-sided
exceedance probability. It remains conservative when the standardized curve
retraces itself. Polynomial knots split integration without adding endpoint
contributions. Exact zero variance and rank-one processes are treated
separately. Integration error estimates are added to the computed length for
a numerical margin; they are not rigorous numerical error certificates.

The probability statement concerns the selected Gaussian coefficient
approximation. Bayesian covariance gives approximate posterior probability;
frequentist covariance calibrates centered Gaussian sampling errors. Neither
choice removes smoothing bias. The unconditional correction uses mgcv's
corrected covariance, rather than integrating a smoothing-parameter posterior.

Coverage is separate for each fitted smooth or factor-level pair. The domain
is a continuous interval, including gaps in the output coordinates. A fixed
domain makes the critical value independent of output density. Rendering the
bounds as connected line segments remains a plotting approximation.

simultaneous_domain accepts two endpoints or a named list keyed by coordinate.
By default it is the evaluation range. A single output point requires an
explicit interval. Derivative stencils must remain in fitted spline support.
tube_control exposes rel_tol, abs_tol, subdivisions, and root_tol. Tube
calibration ignores n_sim, n_cores/ncores, and seed and does not change RNG state.
interval_info records scope, domains, critical values, lengths, component
counts, covariance flags, convergence diagnostics, and derivative settings.

Unsupported bases, surfaces, constrained models, shape enforcement, varying
numeric by multipliers, raw-scale derivatives of transformed coordinates,
coordinate-dependent group contributions, and unresolved near-zero variance
produce explicit errors. They do not trigger an automatic simulation fallback.

Deterministic and regression checks

The tests compare analytic circular-arc lengths and normal critical values;
check coordinate and coefficient-basis invariance; exercise singular and
invalid covariance, exact and near-zero variance, and duplicate subdivisions;
and compare fitted matrices and derivative operators for all five bases.
They also check covariance options, group means and contrast reversal, output
density invariance, RNG preservation, nested estimates and metadata, GAM/BAM/
GAMM/GAMM4 adapters, transformed smooth coordinates, and plotting wrappers.

The single-point contrast regression checks model terms, so a group slope
cannot be mistaken for a constant group contribution. Requests that exclude
group means still work. Subsetting away the smooth label removes per-curve
metadata without a missing-column warning.

Final targeted run: 225 assertions passed, with no failures, warnings, or skips.

Conditional Gaussian coverage

Run: Rscript notes/tube-bands/validate.R
Default: 50,000 draws for each of four quantities (200,000 total), seed 29017.

The reference independently finds continuous maxima of each simulated cubic
process. On each polynomial segment it solves all real roots of
2 p'(x) q(x) - p(x) q'(x), for p(x)/sqrt(q(x)), and checks endpoints. It does
not use the tube metric or an evaluation-grid maximum. For 100 common draws
per quantity, it was cross-checked against a 5,001-point grid: continuous
maxima were at least as large, with discrepancies below 0.01.

Quantity       Tube c     Simulated continuous c    Coverage   95% binomial CI
Smooth         2.791659   2.779203                  0.95178    0.94987--0.95364
Derivative 1   2.802690   2.730431                  0.95938    0.95761--0.96109
Derivative 2   2.699231   2.602733                  0.96068    0.95894--0.96237
Difference     2.790140   2.782616                  0.95132    0.94940--0.95319

All four results agree with the requested conservative Gaussian calibration.
Tightening rel_tol to 1e-9 and abs_tol to 1e-10 changed the critical values by
at most 1.4e-9. conditional-coverage.csv contains unrounded results.

Repeated-fit experiments

Run: Rscript notes/tube-bands/coverage.R
Default: 200 independent refits in each of 10 scenarios (2,000 fitted models).
Each scenario has reproducible seeds. experiment-config.csv records the final
basis sizes, families, and unconditional settings. The raw refits-*.csv files
retain every fit; RUN_REFITS=false regenerates summaries without fitting.

Containment here is assessed on 401 points in [0.02, 0.98], against the known
generating curve or analytic derivatives. This is a test of fixed-function
coverage on a dense grid, not a certified continuous maximum of estimation
error. Smooth targets are centered using the fitted covariate distribution.
Difference targets include the group contributions. Non-Gaussian targets are
on the linear predictor scale.

Scenario                    Smooth    Derivative 1    Derivative 2
Broad Gaussian              0.995     0.985           0.975
Broad, corrected covariance 0.980     0.990           0.990
Nearly linear               0.995     0.970           1.000
Sharp peak, k = 40          0.915     0.965           0.980
Sharp peak, k = 4           0.000     0.000           0.000
Boundary with uneven data   0.985     0.800           0.865
Binomial                    0.975     0.970           0.985
Poisson                     0.970     0.975           0.975

Equal-group difference: 0.975. Different-group difference: 0.950.
All scenarios completed with zero interval computation errors.
refit-coverage.csv includes exact binomial confidence intervals and widths.

The boundary derivative results and inadequate peak basis show that calibrating
the coefficient process does not correct spline approximation or smoothing
bias. Even the larger peak basis had smooth coverage 0.915 (95% binomial CI
0.867--0.950). These results support the Gaussian-process interpretation in
the documentation; they do not support claiming universally nominal fixed-
function coverage. The peak experiment uses k = 40 in the retained final run;
k = 4 is a separate deliberate inadequate-basis experiment.

Performance and plotting

Run: Rscript notes/tube-bands/benchmark.R
Compare cr/tp bases, k = 6/12, n = 100/1,000, smooth/second derivative,
simulation with 10,000 draws, and medians of three repetitions. timings.csv
contains all measurements, which describe this machine and configuration.

For smooths with 1,000 returned points, cr tube times were 0.018--0.028 seconds
versus 0.453--0.613 for simulation; tp tube times were 0.119--0.152 versus
0.474--0.526. For second derivatives at that density, cr tube times were
0.158--0.329 versus 0.482--0.592. Thin plate second derivatives were slower:
3.07--3.55 seconds for tube calibration across the tested densities versus
0.031--0.580 for simulation. Their numerous polynomial boundaries increase
integration work. Tube calibration is therefore not uniformly faster.

tube-plots.png was rendered and visually inspected. Smooth, second derivative,
and difference bands render correctly and preserve existing caption formats.
No numerical diagnostics or calibration commentary were added to captions.

Sources implemented

Sun and Loader (1994), Simultaneous confidence bands for linear regression
and smoothing, Annals of Statistics 22, 1328--1345,
doi:10.1214/aos/1176325631.
Krivobokova, Kneib and Claeskens (2010), Simultaneous confidence bands for
penalized spline estimators, JASA 105, 852--863,
doi:10.1198/jasa.2010.tm09165.
Wiesenfarth et al. (2012), Direct simultaneous inference in additive models
and its application to model undernutrition, JASA 107, 1286--1296.
Liebl and Reimherr (2023), Fast and fair simultaneous confidence bands for
functional parameters, JRSS B 85, 842--868, doi:10.1093/jrsssb/qkad026.

The implementation uses the conservative constant-threshold Gaussian crossing
bound. It does not implement the adaptive thresholds proposed in the last
reference, Student-t tube corrections, or smoothing-bias correction.
