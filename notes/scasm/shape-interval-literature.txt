Intervals for shape-constrained functions: literature and use with scasm()
2026-10-16

Scope

This note records the literature search requested after the implementation of
shape-enforced scasm intervals. It includes the four previously identified
references: Chernozhukov et al. (2009), Chen et al. (2021), Dümbgen (2003), and
Hazelton and Turlach (2011). Full references and primary-source links appear
at the end. The search is focused on alternatives relevant to monotone or
convex regression functions; it is not an exhaustive systematic review.

The discussion separates results established in the papers from the work
proposed here for scasm(). Adaptation requirements are our assessment, not
claims that the cited authors established inference for mgcv's scasm models.
The implementation status was checked against the gratia sources on this date.

Calibration and shape enforcement are separate decisions

Some methods change an existing uncertainty region; others construct one from
the likelihood, local averages, or a posterior model. Endpoint rearrangement
and the more general operators are one family, not two independent methods
of calibrating uncertainty. Neither needs a bootstrap input: an appropriate
Gaussian band can be supplied instead.

For a simultaneous band, the event is L(x) <= f(x) <= U(x) for every x in
the stated domain. A pointwise interval controls coverage at one fixed x.
Repeating pointwise intervals across a grid does not give a simultaneous band.
A Bayesian credible region instead controls posterior probability under its
specified model. These distinctions determine which guarantees transfer.

The following sections compare the statistical requirements as well as the
software work. Reusing a fitted basis or likelihood is not sufficient to
transfer a coverage theorem to a penalized additive model.

1. Transform the endpoints of an existing simultaneous band

References: [1], [2].

Construction and evidence. Apply an operator T to the estimate and the two
endpoint functions. Rearrangement imposes monotonicity; convex minorants and
related operators impose curvature and certain combinations of restrictions.
If T preserves order and fixes functions in the target class, then

    L <= f <= U  implies  T(L) <= T(f) = f <= T(U).

The whole-function coverage event is therefore preserved. The papers also
give contraction results in specified distances. Such a result does not mean
that the width decreases at every location. Transforming marginal pointwise
limits does not generally preserve their marginal coverage probabilities.

Use with scasm(). This is implemented through shape_intervals() and the
interval_method = "shape" integration. The Gaussian source provides an
alternative to stored bootstrap limits. The current transformation handles
nonnegativity, monotonicity in several coordinates, and curvature in at most
one coordinate, on a complete Cartesian evaluation grid. It does not implement
all the operators or multivariate convexity results in [2].

Remaining work. Stronger nominal coverage requires a better justified initial
band, including its treatment of smoothing bias and smoothing parameters.
Extending the guarantee between evaluation points requires a continuum band
or a bound on interpolation error. Additional shape operators need proofs
that their combinations preserve the required properties, then numerical
validation. The transformed values need not belong to the fitted spline space.

2. Intersect a confidence region with restrictions, then optimize bounds

Reference: [3] gives a concrete application to an inverse problem.

Construction and evidence. Let C be an initially calibrated confidence set
and S the known admissible class. For a truth in S,

    f in C intersect S  if and only if  f in C.

Minimizing and maximizing a functional over C intersect S gives bounds that
cover it whenever the set contains the truth. Joint coverage follows for all
such bounds from the same event. Kuusela and Stark combine calibrated data-fit
restrictions with positivity, monotonicity and convexity, and obtain conservative
finite-sample simultaneous intervals in their particle-spectrum model. That
model-specific calibration is distinct from the general intersection argument.

Use with scasm(). The current shape_confidence_set() represents

    {beta: L <= X beta <= U, Ain beta >= bin, C beta = d}.

in_shape_set() checks membership. Construction does not check feasibility,
optimize bounds, sample the set, or extract restrictions from a fitted model.

Remaining work. Extract the effective coefficient restrictions, equalities,
and prediction matrix in the same parameterization. Add feasibility checks
and reliable optimization of a' beta. A band box with linear restrictions
gives linear programs; an ellipsoidal initial region requires a different
region representation and convex optimization. Handle singular covariance in
estimable coordinates, unbounded objectives, scaling, and solver tolerances.
Report numerical certificates or residuals with each computed bound.

Statistical requirement. Establish calibration of the initial region for the
target coefficients or function. An ellipsoid made from the same approximate
Gaussian covariance does not itself resolve smoothing bias or behavior when
constraints bind. Separate pointwise limits do not establish joint calibration.

Unlike endpoint transformations, extrema over the intersected band box cannot
extend beyond the original limits at those evaluation points. The two extremal
curves need not themselves satisfy coupled restrictions or integral equalities;
they summarize a set of admissible whole functions.

3. Specify and sample a constrained Bayesian spline posterior

Reference: [4].

Construction and evidence. Hazelton and Turlach use the mixed-model
representation of penalized splines, restrict prior support through truncation,
and fit using MCMC with truncated multivariate normal proposals. Posterior
draws give uncertainty under a model in which the functions satisfy the shape
restrictions. Pointwise quantiles give credible intervals; a simultaneous
credible band requires joint posterior calibration. Frequentist coverage is
a separate property to investigate.

Use with scasm(). The spline basis, penalties, family likelihood, and effective
linear restrictions are useful inputs. A fitted scasm object would provide a
model specification and starting values, rather than the required posterior.

Remaining work. Specify priors for all coefficients, smoothing parameters and
any dispersion or random-effect parameters. Implement a sampler respecting
inequalities and identifiability equalities, including the dependence of a
truncated prior's normalizing constant on its hyperparameters where relevant.
Parameterizing equalities explicitly can reduce the sampling dimension.
Validate posterior calculations, mixing, effective sample sizes, and interval
construction. Extend beyond the linear mixed-model setting only with a suitable
likelihood and sampler. Choose whether inference conditions on smoothing or
integrates over it, and document that choice.

Simply truncating Gaussian draws centered at the constrained estimate does
not establish that they sample this posterior. Likewise, the current Gaussian
source followed by endpoint transformation does not fit a constrained posterior.

4. Construct simultaneous bands using multiscale inequalities

References: [5], [6]; the surface extension is [11].

Construction and evidence. Local averages at multiple locations and scales
give one-sided information under the shape restriction. Joint noise bounds
turn those inequalities into simultaneous bands. Dümbgen establishes calibrated
bands for monotone and convex functions in Gaussian white noise. Yang and
Barber give a different construction using isotonic least squares and local
averages, with finite-sample bands for independent sub-Gaussian errors at the
specified noise bound. Estimating that bound requires its own justification.

Datta, Mukherjee and Sen extend multiscale bands to multidimensional white
noise, including coordinatewise monotonicity and convexity. Their guarantees
are in that observation model, not automatically in tensor-product GAMs.

Use with scasm(). Start with a univariate Gaussian regression problem and a
specified noise model. These methods construct a separate uncertainty region;
they do not take the existing smooth standard errors as their calibration.

Remaining work. Implement the local statistics and critical values for the
chosen observation model and design. For additive models, account for error
in estimating the intercept and other smooths; treating fitted partial
residuals as independent observations would require justification. Non-Gaussian
families need likelihood-appropriate inequalities. Specify whether the target
is a link-scale component or a response-scale mean. Surface support also needs
multidimensional local averaging and critical-value calculations, and a clear
distinction between directional and joint convexity. Validate the claimed
coverage in the discrete model actually used by the software.

5. Invert shape-restricted likelihood-ratio tests

References: [7], [8].

Construction and evidence. For each candidate theta at x0, compare the best
shape-constrained likelihood with the best likelihood additionally satisfying
f(x0) = theta. Invert the test to obtain an interval. Banerjee and Wellner
develop this for current-status data; Banerjee treats a broader class of
monotone response models, including Gaussian, binary and Poisson examples.
The pointwise likelihood-ratio limit is nonstandard, not the usual chi-squared
law. Its regularity conditions matter, including the local behavior of the
monotone function at the interior target point.

Use with scasm(). Prediction rows supply the candidate-value equality and
the fitted constraints supply part of the optimization problem. However,
the cited theory concerns the shape-restricted nonparametric likelihood,
not a generic penalized scasm likelihood.

Remaining work. Implement repeated constrained profiling with nuisance terms,
candidate-value equalities, and stable interval-endpoint searches. Decide
whether the criterion is the likelihood or penalized likelihood and how
smoothing parameters are selected during profiling. Derive or otherwise
justify critical values for that exact construction, especially at flat
regions or binding restrictions. Convex regression needs its own theory;
the monotone likelihood-ratio theorem is not a general curvature result.
Simultaneous bands would require joint calibration beyond these pointwise tests.

6. Construct adaptive pointwise intervals using shape-controlled bias

Reference: [9].

Construction and evidence. Cai, Low and Xia use local estimates whose biases
have known signs under monotonicity or convexity, then select a scale using
the data. They establish coverage over the assumed shape class and expected
lengths that adapt to the particular function. Their settings include white
noise and equally spaced Gaussian regression with known noise level. These
are intervals for a fixed function value, not simultaneous bands.

Use with scasm(). The method is best introduced first as a separate univariate
interval procedure. It does not need the fitted spline covariance and may
produce intervals that are not centered at the scasm estimate.

Remaining work. Implement the one-sided local estimates and the prescribed
scale selection. Establish how to handle the intended design, unknown variance,
and nuisance additive components. A residual-based substitution needs analysis
of the nuisance estimation error and dependence it introduces. For other
families or surfaces, derive suitable local estimates and coverage results.
Providing a plotted grid of intervals must retain the pointwise interpretation
unless a separate simultaneous construction is supplied.

7. Normalize the errors of a shape-constrained estimator by its local geometry

Reference: [10].

Construction and evidence. Deng, Han and Sen use the lengths of local linear
pieces of the convex least-squares estimator to normalize estimation errors.
The resulting limiting distributions give asymptotically exact intervals for
function values and derivatives at an interior point under their assumptions.
The canonical curvature case has positive second derivative there; the
paper also treats specified higher-order local behavior. A locally affine
truth is not covered merely by invoking the positive-curvature result.

Use with scasm(). The natural convex least-squares estimator is piecewise
linear and differs from a penalized spline. Its local segment lengths and
critical values cannot simply be attached to a scasm estimate.

Remaining work. Either implement that separate estimator and its interval
procedure, or establish a corresponding limit theorem for the penalized
spline estimator, including its basis dimension and smoothing regime.
The separate implementation needs segment detection, noise estimation and
the relevant critical values. Extending it to additive nuisance terms,
non-Gaussian responses, or surfaces requires further statistical development.
The cited intervals remain pointwise unless joint calibration is added.

Requirements shared by proposed scasm integrations

Define the inferential target first: a smooth component, the complete linear
predictor, a response-scale mean, a derivative, or an integral. Monotonicity
can transfer through an increasing inverse link, but convexity generally
does not. Component restrictions do not automatically restrict a sum of
components or a surface after arbitrary shifts and transformations.

Use the constraints that the model actually fitted. Translate them through
centering, coefficient reparameterization, tensor transformations and factor-by
construction. Check that they imply the named function shape: raw second
differences with irregular knots, for example, need not ensure convexity.
Some tensor reparameterizations can also invalidate an inference from
marginal coefficient restrictions to the named surface shape. The concrete
counterexamples and present eligibility rules are in shape-method-review.txt.

Separate uncertainty for coefficients within a chosen spline space from
coverage for an underlying regression function. Spline approximation error,
smoothing bias, smoothing-parameter selection, and binding constraints need
to be treated by the proposed calibration rather than assumed away by
reusing a covariance matrix.

Validation should include flat and linear truths, nearly binding constraints,
interior and boundary evaluation points, irregular designs and knots, nuisance
smooths, and supported tensor constructions. Assess pointwise and simultaneous
coverage separately and report Monte Carlo uncertainty. Include fitting or
solver failures in the accounting. Numerical checks should cover feasibility,
ordering, constraint residuals, conditioning, and optimization or sampling
accuracy as appropriate to the method.

Recommended order of development

The nearest software extension is optimization over the existing intersected
set, initially using a supplied simultaneous band box and linear constraints.
Its coverage claim remains conditional on that supplied band's calibration.
An ellipsoidal region is a useful subsequent representation, with a separate
assessment of how it is calibrated for scasm.

A constrained Bayesian posterior is the most direct route that retains the
penalized-spline model while incorporating the restrictions into uncertainty
from the outset. It is a model-fitting project as well as an interval feature.

For a direct frequentist construction, investigate multiscale bands first in
univariate Gaussian regression, where the observation model can be matched
explicitly. The likelihood-ratio, adaptive local and normalized-error methods
are valuable alternatives for pointwise inference, but each needs more than
an interface to the existing smooth covariance.
