API and implementation

shape_intervals() accepts estimates, existing endpoints, numeric evaluation
coordinates and named shape restrictions independently of a fitted model.
It applies nonnegative range enforcement, directional rearrangement, then
one directional convex minorant or concave majorant. Curvature uses actual
coordinate distances. The operators preserve order and fix targets in the
named shape class; maximum absolute distances cannot increase. Therefore an
initial band covering the whole evaluation grid continues to cover that
target after transformation. Maximum width cannot increase, although width
or marginal coverage at an individual point may change.

This finite-grid argument follows the operators studied by Chernozhukov,
Fernandez-Val and Galichon (2009) and Chen et al. (2021). The independent
review in shape-method-review.txt gives proofs and counterexamples for the
precise finite-grid choices used here. On irregular grids rearrangement
assigns equal mass to coordinate values, rather than approximating a uniform
measure on the continuous domain. Endpoint interpolation preserves the
named directional shapes but does not extend coverage between nodes.

shape_confidence_set() explicitly represents
  {b: lower <= X b <= upper, Ain b >= bin, C b = d}.
in_shape_set() checks candidate coefficients. These functions retain coupled
restrictions and equalities instead of assigning them to endpoint curves.
They do not optimize over the set, test emptiness, or sample a posterior.
Intersection preserves the initial whole-grid coverage event exactly for
feasible truths. A posterior-probability interpretation requires a posterior
supported on the stated restrictions.

smooth_estimates(), confint(), draw() and assemble() expose the operators
through interval_method = "shape". shape_source = "gaussian" is the new
method's default initial source; "bootstrap" uses stored native pointwise
limits and their existing svar.only correction. Existing interval defaults
are unchanged. Source, correction, shape, coordinate scale, SE covariance
and coverage metadata are retained. Saved, nested and subset estimates draw
their stored endpoints; add_confint() replaces them with Gaussian pointwise
limits and removes the shape-enforcement metadata.

The default Gaussian covariance is an approximate Bayesian covariance, not
a posterior restricted to feasible functions. frequentist selects Ve, and
unconditional retains the existing Gaussian covariance selection. Plotting
now forwards frequentist for smooth components. Parametric intervals retain
their existing covariance calculation. Native bootstrap shape limits reject
frequentist or unconditional requests, since stored draws hold smoothing
fixed and the native endpoint correction uses the Bayesian/frequentist SE gap.

Eligibility and explicit omissions

Automatic model integration requires retained named scasm restrictions for
every selected component. Univariate B-splines and safe te() tensors are
supported, including factor-by components and separate linear predictors.
Tensors require np = FALSE and univariate nonnegative B-spline margins;
curvature requires regular fitted knots and degree at least two. Evaluation
must remain inside their basis domains, on complete finite product grids.
Use dist = 0 for tensors. Missing rows and duplicate grid rows are rejected.
Transformed covariates are evaluated normally; shape concerns model coordinates.

The stricter tensor rule matters: installed mgcv 1.9-4 can retain coefficient
inequalities while its default normal reparameterisation permits decreasing
function slices. Irregular fitted knots can likewise invalidate a named
curvature restriction. Independently verified numerical counterexamples are
recorded in shape-method-review.txt. The engine rejects these automatic uses
instead of inferring function shapes from inequality retention alone.

Monotonicity may be enforced in several directions; curvature is limited to
one direction. A second directional convexification can destroy the first.
General coupled inequalities and equalities use the explicit set API.
Numeric by variables, overall mean uncertainty, effect shifts and effect
transformations are unsupported for automatic shape intervals. Full fitted
values and derivatives retain their existing interval methods; their shapes
need separate derivations before automatic integration. Several extreme
numeric ranges are rejected when reliable slope comparisons or interpolation
cannot be performed in double precision.

No nominal coverage is established by endpoint enforcement itself. Pointwise
sources warn because marginal coverage can fall after the transformation.
Smoothing bias and inaccurate uncertainty near binding constraints remain
properties of the initial calculation. Feasible native bootstrap draws alone
do not establish coverage. A constrained Bayesian sampler, ellipsoidal initial
regions, bound optimization and feasibility optimization are separate future
work. The sampler would need an explicitly restricted posterior, as in the
approach of Hazelton and Turlach (2011). Dümbgen's (2003) multiscale calibration
for a Gaussian white-noise experiment does not directly supply calibration
for these penalized GAMs. Andrews (2000) explains the bootstrap boundary issue.

Reproducible simulation

Rscript notes/scasm/shape-coverage.R
SHAPE_BOUNDARY_ONLY=1 Rscript notes/scasm/shape-coverage.R

The fixed seed is 20261016. There are 2,000 repetitions for each of nine
known-Gaussian truths (18,000 total), and 400 attempted scasm fits for each
of six truths (2,400 total). The latter include nearly and exactly binding
monotonicity/curvature restrictions. Known-Gaussian simultaneous bands use
exact Sidak calibration on the listed grids. scasm bands compare Gaussian
Vp/Ve calculations, native corrected and raw bootstrap percentiles, and a
small fixed penalty for examining smoothing sensitivity. Gaussian scasm
critical values use 1,999 normal draws; native bootstrap uses 399 draws.
The scasm experiment applies the low-level operator to fitted response curves
including their intercepts, giving each simulated truth a fixed reference
level. Automatic integration returns smooth components as described above.

At 95% coverage, binomial Monte Carlo SE is about 0.49 percentage points for
2,000 repetitions and 1.09 for 400 successful fits. Worst-case SE is 1.12
and 2.50 respectively. CSVs record actual source-specific SEs and counts;
critical-value simulation error is additional. Across 49,182 paired interval
transformations, using 1e-10 tolerance for numerical comparisons, no originally
covering whole-grid band lost coverage, no
maximum width increased, and no limits became unordered. Independent
all-bracketing-chord convex calculations agreed within 2.22e-16.

This does not rescue pointwise calibration. With known independent normal
errors and a nearly flat increasing truth, minimum marginal coverage changed
from 93.65% to 38.00% after transforming pointwise limits. With an initially
simultaneous band, whole-grid coverage changed from 94.85% to 95.75%. For a
nearly linear convex truth, mean marginal coverage of transformed pointwise
limits was 73.01%, with minimum 57.30%.

For the nearly binding increasing-convex scasm truth, transformed Gaussian
simultaneous coverage was 313/342 = 91.52%, Monte Carlo SE 1.51 percentage
points. The other 58 fit attempts raised an error. Exactly flat and exactly
linear truths gave corresponding coverage of 332/342 = 97.08% (SE 0.91) and
344/361 = 95.29% (SE 1.11). These estimates are conditional on a returned
model. Returned models with warnings remain in summaries. Across the six
scenarios there were 203 upstream fitting errors and 156 fitting warnings;
the CSVs preserve all messages, including a convergence warning. No fitting
error was patched or replaced with a successful result.

For the curved increasing example, maximum absolute empirical bias changed
from 0.0656 with selected smoothing to 0.0277 with sp = 1e-8, while RMSE changed
from 0.0416 to 0.0466. This compares bias/variance within the same basis;
constraints and finite-basis approximation still contribute.

Full simulation tables, source counts, binding frequencies and qualifications
are in shape-coverage-validation.txt and shape-coverage-boundary-validation.txt,
with matching summary/failure/warning/binding CSVs. Raw replication CSVs remain
available locally and are ignored by Git; the script reproduces them.

the repository's socket-permission instructions. Public topics were added to
the pkgdown reference index. The article's runnable univariate/tensor examples
and HTML rendering passed. The generated plots were inspected visually.
